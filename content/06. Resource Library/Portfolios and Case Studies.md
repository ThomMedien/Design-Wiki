# Portfolios and Case Studies

A design portfolio is more than just a collection of pretty pictures; it's a testament to your problem-solving skills, your process, and your impact as a designer. Case studies are the heart of this narrative.

## What a Portfolio Should Show

### 1. Your Process
Don't just show the final result. Documentation of your journey—from initial sketches and wireframes to high-fidelity prototypes—demonstrates how you think and how you arrive at solutions.

### 2. Problem-Solving Abilities
Every project starts with a problem. Clearly define what you were trying to solve, the constraints you faced, and how your design addresses those specific challenges.

### 3. Impact and Results
Whenever possible, include metrics or qualitative feedback. Did your redesign increase conversion? Did the new UI improve user satisfaction scores? Tangible results validate your work.

### 4. Technical Proficiency
Showcase your mastery of tools (Adobe Creative Cloud, Figma, etc.) and your understanding of design fundamentals like typography, color theory, and grid systems.

## Structuring an Effective Case Study

1.  **Overview:** A brief summary of the project, your role, and the timeline.
2.  **The Challenge:** What was the core problem? Who was the target audience?
3.  **Research & Discovery:** How did you understand the users and the market?
4.  **Ideation & Strategy:** Show your brainstorming, user flows, and early concepts.
5.  **Execution:** Present the final designs with explanations for key decisions.
6.  **Reflection:** What did you learn? What would you do differently next time?

## Key Takeaways
- **Quality over Quantity:** Focus on 3-5 strong, diverse projects.
- **Narrative is Key:** Tell a story that keeps the viewer engaged.
- **Accessibility:** Ensure your portfolio itself is a model of good UX/UI.
