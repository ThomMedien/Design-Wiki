# Media Law (Medienrecht)

Media law is a compulsory subject in the written IHK exam, focusing on intellectual property, data protection, privacy, and website regulation.

---

## 1. Copyright Law (Urheberrecht)

German Copyright Law (*Urheberrechtsgesetz - UrhG*) protects creators of literary, scientific, and artistic works.

### Key Principles:
*   **Automatic Protection:** Copyright protection arises automatically at the moment of creation. Registration (like the © symbol in the US) is not required.
*   **Schöpfungshöhe (Threshold of Originality):** A work is only protected if it is a personal intellectual creation (*persönliche geistige Schöpfung*) showing a sufficient degree of creativity. Technical layouts or pure data tables may not qualify.
*   **Duration:** Protection lasts until **70 years after the creator's death** (*70 Jahre post mortem auctoris*). After that, the work becomes public domain (*gemeinfrei*).
*   **Inalienability:** Under German law, the copyright itself cannot be sold or transferred. Only **usage rights** can be granted.

### Types of Usage Rights (Nutzungsrechte):
To use someone else's work, you must obtain usage rights (§ 31 UrhG):
*   **Einfaches Nutzungsrecht (Simple Right):** The creator can license the work to multiple parties (e.g., standard stock photos).
*   **Ausschließliches Nutzungsrecht (Exclusive Right):** Only the licensee has the right to use the work. The creator cannot license it to others.
*   **Limitations:** Rights can be restricted by:
    *   **Space (Räumlich):** e.g., only valid in Germany.
    *   **Time (Zeitlich):** e.g., valid for 2 years.
    *   **Purpose (Inhaltlich):** e.g., only for print flyers, not for television ads.

## 2. Right to One's Own Image (Recht am eigenen Bild)

Protects personal privacy regarding photographs and video recordings. It is regulated by the **Kunsturhebergesetz (KunstUrhG)**.

### General Rule:
Images of individuals may only be distributed or displayed with their explicit consent (§ 22 KunstUrhG). If the subject is paid (e.g., a professional model), consent is legally assumed.

### Exceptions (§ 23 KunstUrhG):
You can publish photos *without* consent in four specific scenarios:
1.  **Bildnisse aus dem Bereich der Zeitgeschichte:** Public figures or events of historical/contemporary significance (e.g., politicians, major news events). However, their private sphere remains protected.
2.  **Personen als Beiwerk (Accessory):** The person is merely an accessory in a photo of a landscape or landmark. If the person is cropped out and the image still works, they are likely an accessory.
3.  **Versammlungen & Aufzüge (Assemblies):** Images showing demonstrations, concerts, or public gatherings where individuals are part of the crowd.
4.  **Höheres Kunstinteresse:** Non-commissioned artistic photographs of high cultural value.

## 3. Web Regulations: Imprint & Privacy

### Impressumspflicht (Imprint Duty)
According to **§ 5 of the Digitale-Dienste-Gesetz (DDG)** (formerly TMG), all commercial online services must display an easily recognizable, directly accessible, and permanently available imprint.
*   **Who needs it?** Almost all websites. Only purely private, family-oriented websites are exempt.
*   **Required Information:**
    *   Full name and physical address (not a P.O. Box).
    *   Fast contact info: Email address and a phone number (or rapid contact form).
    *   Legal representation (e.g., CEO for a GmbH).
    *   Commercial register and register number (if registered).
    *   VAT Identification Number (*USt-IdNr*) if applicable.

### GDPR (DSGVO - Datenschutz-Grundverordnung)
Any website operating in the EU collecting personal data (IP addresses, names, emails) must comply:
*   **Datenschutzerklärung:** A detailed, readable privacy policy must be accessible in one click.
*   **Cookie Consent:** Users must actively consent (Opt-In) before non-essential tracking cookies (e.g., Google Analytics) are loaded (ruling of the ECJ on Planet49).
*   **HTTPS Encryption:** Mandatory for websites that transmit user data via input forms (e.g., contact forms).
