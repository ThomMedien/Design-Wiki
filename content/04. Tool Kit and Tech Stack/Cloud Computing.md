# Cloud Computing

## What is Cloud Computing?
At its core, cloud computing is the on-demand delivery of IT resources—including servers, storage, databases, networking, and software—over the internet. Instead of owning and maintaining physical data centers and servers, you "rent" computing power and storage from providers.

## The "Big Three" Providers
1. **Amazon Web Services (AWS):** The market pioneer and leader. It offers the most extensive range of services and global infrastructure, making it the default choice for many enterprises and high-growth startups.
2. **Microsoft Azure:** Strongest in corporate environments already using the Microsoft ecosystem (Windows, Office 365, Active Directory). It is highly integrated and offers excellent hybrid cloud capabilities.
3. **Google Cloud Platform (GCP):** Known for its strengths in high-level computing, data analytics, and Machine Learning. It is often the choice for companies focused on big data and AI.

## Why It’s Important
- **Scalability:** You can scale up (add more power) or scale out (add more instances) instantly to handle traffic spikes, and scale back down to save money.
- **Cost Efficiency:** It shifts IT spending from **CapEx** (Capital Expenditure - buying hardware) to **OpEx** (Operating Expenditure - paying for what you use).
- **Agility:** Teams can spin up environments in minutes rather than waiting weeks for hardware procurement.
- **Reliability:** Massive infrastructure providers offer levels of uptime and data redundancy that are impossible for most individual companies to achieve.

## Business Context: Startups
For a startup, cloud computing is a "force multiplier":
- **Low Barrier to Entry:** You can launch a global application for a few dollars a month.
- **Focus on Core Product:** Engineers spend time building features rather than managing server hardware or cooling systems.
- **"Fail Fast" Capability:** If a product idea doesn't work, you simply delete the cloud resources and stop paying for them immediately.
- **Global Reach:** A startup in a garage can deploy their app to data centers in London, Tokyo, and New York with a single command.
